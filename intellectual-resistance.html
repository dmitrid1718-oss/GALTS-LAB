Can AI help a person become more capable without doing the growing for them?

A proposed experiment

The question

AI can make a difficult task easier to finish. This experiment asks a different question: which kind of help leaves a person better able to do the task independently afterward?

Working hypothesis, not an established finding: Meaningful effort, well-timed feedback, and recovery may help build lasting capability. The right amount of challenge may matter; more difficulty is not automatically better.

This is a research proposal. No participant results have been collected, and the idea should be tested against alternatives.

The working loop

Support the learner. Keep the thinking theirs.

01 · ATTEMPT

Try before asking

Choose a meaningful, manageable skill. Make an independent first attempt and record what feels uncertain.

02 · RESISTANCE

Get a useful challenge

The AI asks for reasoning, offers a counterexample, or gives a graduated hint. It does not immediately take over the task.

03 · RECOVERY

Pause and reflect

Record what changed, what remains unclear, and whether the challenge felt productive or excessive. Rest is part of the design.

04 · RETEST

Return without AI

Try a new problem independently. The test is what the learner can now do, explain, and transfer without assistance.

A possible 14-day pilot

Compare approaches fairly.

Invite volunteers to choose one skill they actually want to improve. Compare guided intellectual resistance with ordinary AI tutoring and self-directed study. Keep learning time and starting difficulty as similar as practical.

What to measure

  • Independent performance before and after
  • Retention after two and seven days without assistance
  • Transfer to an unfamiliar problem
  • Time invested and learner-reported burden

What could disprove it?

  • Ordinary tutoring produces equal or better independent results
  • Any gain disappears after a short delay
  • Extra challenge increases burden without improving transfer
  • Self-directed practice performs just as well
Guardrails: Participation should be voluntary. Use a low-stakes skill, keep the challenge manageable, and stop if it becomes harmful or overwhelming. This is not clinical or professional advice.
The Galt question

What should AI make easier?

Removing needless toil can expand a person's freedom. Removing every opportunity to practice judgment may narrow it. The experiment asks where that line falls in one small, observable task—and welcomes evidence that the premise is wrong.

Open question: What skill would make a good first test because progress can be measured without confusing AI-completed work with human capability?
